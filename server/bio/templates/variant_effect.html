How to read this. The delta says whether and what kind, never how much: a Δ of 0.9 does not distinguish 10% of transcripts from 90%. It is also not a pathogenicity score. On ClinVar the meta layer ties the base model (ROC-AUC 0.751 vs 0.754), because ~89% of pathogenic ClinVar variants act by non-splicing mechanisms. Where the meta layer does win is consequence type: on the 434 RNA-seq-validated MutSpliceDB variants, M2-S agrees with the observed effect 72.1% of the time vs M1-S 50.7%, and it recovers 10 of the 12 that the base model scores below 0.1 (M1-S recovers 4).

Scores are not comparable across models at a fixed cutoff. At |Δ| > 0.8 the base model flags 76.5% of validated variants and M2-S only 48.2%, which is calibration, not blindness. Compare each model against its own scale.

Where the meta layer is worse: placing the site

Naming the change and locating it are different skills, and they come apart here. Measured over 409 MutSpliceDB variants whose disrupted site is derivable from the HGVS offset and the MANE annotation: does the model's largest loss land on that site? Stratified by how far the site is from the variant, because most splice-site variants sit right on top of it and that case is easy for everyone.
